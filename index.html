HERO CASE STUDY

Panfacial Trauma Reconstruction

Management of Bilateral ZMC Fractures Concomitant with Bilateral Subcondylar and Right Parasymphysis Fractures

1. Emergency Presentation & Clinical Assessment

History of Present Illness (HPI): A 24-year-old male patient presented to the emergency trauma bay following an alleged high-velocity Road Traffic Accident (RTA). The patient was traveling on a motorcycle when he collided with an oncoming two-wheeler, resulting in a direct, high-impact deceleration fall face-first onto the ground. A transient loss of consciousness (LOC) of approximately 15 minutes was noted immediately post-impact. Medico-Legal Case (MLC) protocols were activated upon admission.

Clinical Examination (On Examination): Gross facial asymmetry characterized by distinct structural flattening of the left malar profile. Severe soft-tissue edema and tracking ecchymosis localized across the upper lip vestibule, accompanied by prominent left periorbital edema and subconjunctival hemorrhage. Step-deformities were acutely palpated along the left supraorbital ridge margin and the bilateral infraorbital rims. Maxillary skeletal mobility was elicited under manual digital pressure. Intraoral assessment revealed severe trismus and an acutely deranged, unstable dental occlusion featuring premature posterior open contacts and an anterior open bite profile.

2. Pre-Operative Stabilization & Resident Protocols

Airway Security & Monitoring: Due to the loss of anterior mandibular support driven by the bilateral subcondylar skeletal fractures, the patient was at high risk for mechanical airway compromise from posterior tongue relapse. Continuous bedside oxygen saturation tracking was mandated, and an emergency tongue-stitch instrument kit alongside a tracheostomy tray was secured directly at the head of the bed.

Temporary Maxillomandibular Fixation (MMF): Under local infiltration anesthesia (2% Lignocaine Hydrochloride with 1:200,000 Adrenaline), maxillary and mandibular Erich arch bars were adapted and wired. Passive intermaxillary traction was engaged to re-establish a temporary, gross occlusal baseline, neutralize agonizing muscular spasms, and control active intraosseous microvascular bleeding at the mobile right parasymphysis fracture margins.

3. Surgical Sequencing & Hardware Execution

Sequencing Philosophy: To systematically reconstruct the three-dimensional facial dimensions safely, a strict "Bottom-Up and Outside-In" surgical sequence was planned under General Anesthesia (GA), utilizing armored nasotracheal intubation to preserve unhindered access to the dental arches.

  • Step 1: Foundational Occlusal Lock - The displaced mandibular segments were manually reduced into maximum intercuspation and temporarily locked into rigid MMF using wire loops around the pre-placed Erich arch bars.
  • Step 2: Mandibular Base Reconstruction - Approached via a lower anterior mandibular vestibular incision spanning from 35 to 44, with careful subperiosteal isolation of the right mental nerve. Fixation was executed along Champy's lines using a 2.5mm straight 6-hole plate with 8mm bicortical screws at the inferior border, and a secondary 2.0mm straight 6-hole plate with gap at the subapical tension zone.
  • Step 3: Restoring Posterior Vertical Ramus Height - Accessed via a left preauricular incision. Layer-wise dissection exposed the TMJ capsule and condylar process. The low subcondylar fracture was anatomically reduced and fixed using a 2.0mm 4-hole c̅ gap miniplate to lock the vertical ramus projection, followed by a controlled release of the intraoperative wire MMF.
  • Step 4: Midface Framework Realignment - Upper midface frames were approached via bilateral upper maxillary vestibular incisions (13-15 and 23-26 zones) and open reduction through the existing left supraorbital laceration. Plating infrastructure used: Left FZ (1.5mm 4-hole c̅ gap plate), Left Maxillary buttress (2.0mm 6-hole c̅ gap L-shape plate), Nasomaxillary buttress (2.0mm 4-hole c̅ gap straight plate), and Right Zygomatic buttress (2.0mm 4-hole c̅ gap straight plate).

Figure 2: Intraoral layer closure using interrupted 3-0 Vicryl sutures alongside multi-vector orange latex training elastics. (Upload 'arch_bar.jpg' to display).

4. Postoperative Intensive Care & Ward Routine

Following uneventful extubation, the patient was transitioned to the OT-ICU and managed with a 30° propped-up head elevation and neutral positioning to minimize midfacial interstitial lymphedema. Fluid lines consisted of alternating 1L NS and 1L RL run slowly over 4 hours. Pharmacotherapy profile included: Inj. Augmentin 1.2g IV BD, Inj. Metrogyl 100cc IV TDS, Inj. Dexona 8mg IV TDS (anti-edema), Inj. Dynapar 75mg IV BD, Inj. Emset 4mg IV TDS, Inj. Pantocid 40mg IV OD, Inj. Neurobion Forte IV BD, and Inj. Levepsy 500mg IV BD (post-traumatic seizure prophylaxis secondary to history of LOC).
